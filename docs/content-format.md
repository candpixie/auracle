# Content format

## The rule

**You hook once, on day 1. After that the series is the hook.**

Once the premise exists, every later video inherits it. The corner counter does the
framing work so the script never has to. Adding a manufactured hook to a teaching day
just delays the value and costs retention.

## Why this format beats the standard "day N/30" one

The usual version promises a self-transformation: "can I become [status] in [time]."
The subject is the person, nothing is being made, and after three posts the grid says
the same sentence with a different number. Only the counter carries new information.

This one promises an **answer to a question about how hearing works**. The subject is
the thing, not the person, and every single day has a new promise.

And the structural advantage: **abstract ML concepts cannot be demonstrated, audio ones
can.** Gradient descent cannot be heard. A missing fundamental can be heard in one
second. So the demo is the cold open. That is not a hook bolted on the front, it is the
knowledge itself, front-loaded.

## Thumbnail

Two layers.

**Fixed anchor** — small, corner, identical every post:

```
AURACLE  ·  teaching a machine to hear  ·  06/17
```

**Rotating hook** — big, top, different every day, always one concrete perceptual fact.

**Background: the day's actual output.** A CQT plot, a self-similarity matrix, a
spectrogram, a chroma-gram. These are genuinely beautiful, they are being generated
anyway, and they make the format impossible to copy: nobody else's visual layer is
their own scientific output. Face in the lower third, plot behind, anchor bottom,
hook top.

## Video structure, days 2 through 17

```
0:00-0:03   the phenomenon. sound plays, or the figure appears. no setup line.
0:03-0:10   "what you just heard was..."
0:10-0:40   the mechanism. why it happens.
0:40-0:50   where it breaks.
end         no outro, no recap, no "follow for more." end on the failure.
```

Day 1 is the one exception. Premise-heavy is correct there, exactly once.

## The 17 hooks

| Day | Hook |
|-----|------|
| 01 | this sound is lying to you |
| 02 | you can't know *when* and *what* at the same time |
| 03 | these two sounds are identical. your ear disagrees. |
| 04 | why a piano looks wrong to a computer |
| 05 | the standard tool for music was built to ignore music |
| 06 | **you're about to hear a note that isn't there** |
| 07 | the computer thinks this song is half as fast |
| 08 | the hardest thing your ears do, you've never noticed |
| 09 | pulling one voice out of a song |
| 10 | the computer can't tell happy from sad |
| 11 | teaching a computer to find the chorus |
| 12 | i wrote a fourier transform by hand |
| 13 | genre isn't real, and the model is learning that |
| 14 | trained it for six hours. a pretrained model beat it in ten minutes. |
| 15 | can your spotify tell people who you are? |
| 16 | **i tested whether any of this beat random guessing** |
| 17 | 17 days. here's what actually worked. |

Days 6 and 16 are the two big ones. Day 6 makes people hear an illusion through their
phone speaker, which is a share mechanic that talking-head formats do not have. Day 10
is literally true: relative major and minor share a pitch-class set, so key-detection
algorithms confuse them constantly.

## Feed vs stories

17 daily feed posts fights the 2-per-week charter. Instead:

- **Daily to stories.** Low effort, builds the "she is actually doing this" narrative.
- **Five feed videos**, at days 4, 6, 8, 12, 16.

Keep `/17`. The clock creates return visits, and it belongs to every challenge series
ever made, not to any one creator.
